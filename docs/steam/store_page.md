# Gacha Garage — Steam store page

## Short description (shown next to the capsule, max 300 characters)

Grandpa left you his garage, a key, and a pile of notes. Turn the dusty back-lane garage into the best capsule-toy shop in town: fix up old machines, haggle for second-hand stock, pay the electricity bill, and try not to slap the neighbours. Mostly.

## About this game

It's spring, 1999. You drive Grandpa's rusty kei van into a narrow back lane somewhere in Morio-chō, park it in front of a shutter you haven't seen since you were nine, and find an envelope taped to it. It says KIDDO.

Inside is the garage, a fuse box that doesn't work, a cash tin with ¥2,500 in it, and a list. Grandpa's lists are how this game talks to you. Flip the breaker. Air the place out. Feed the cat (she's shy). Earn enough walking a neighbour's shiba to buy a used gacha machine off a website that still runs on dial-up.

**Run a real little shop**

- Every machine is a physical thing. Unlock the lid, cut the tape on the carton, drop capsules in one at a time, swap the display card, lock it again.
- Set the price on each machine's plate. Go over what the neighbourhood thinks is fair and people tell you, loudly, and write it in your guestbook.
- Coins pile up in the coin box. Nobody puts them in your wallet for you.
- Bills come on the 10th and the 27th. If you don't pay the electricity, the lights go out mid-shift.
- Day turns to night. Close the shutter, flip the sign, watch the sunset, read the day's receipt.

**Buy, sell, haggle**

- Your PC runs Capsule 98, and you actually sit down at it. Order new stock from the wholesaler or dig through Off-Hobby, a second-hand site full of figures with stickers, doodled moustaches, sun damage and sellers who are absolutely not scamming you.
- Haggle over chat before the other buyer does. Send rare figures off for grading and get them back in a slab.
- Everything you order comes in a cream van with FREE CANDY painted on the side. The driver is a very nice old man. He remembers your name and brings you a sweet every time. It's fine.

**A lane full of people**

- Neighbours with names, routines and long memories. Ring their bells, eat ramen with them, do their odd jobs.
- A manga artist who wants to draw your face, two schoolboys with very particular feelings about hair, a man who lives in a bush, a pirate who is made of rubber, and a kappa who would like a part-time job.
- After dark, yokai come out. Some of them want to wrestle.
- And there's the little alley beside the corner shop. You're allowed to walk out of it. Just don't look back.

**176 figures in 22 series**

Mochi animals, kaiju eating taiyaki, cats on a space station, Shōwa appliances, sentai heroes on a lunch break, a whole series of extremely bizarre poses, and a box of toys you can actually play with: a foam-dart pistol, a squishy, a wind-up robot, a kendama. Secrets are 1 in 500. Good luck.

**Grow it**

Hire staff from a job site called Inspired. Buy the dead tobacco shop next door. Take out a loan you probably shouldn't. One day, if you keep the lights on, there's a building in town with escalators and four floors of capsule machines with your name on the door.

## Features (bullet list for the sidebar)

- First-person capsule-toy shop sim in a hand-made Shōwa back lane
- Voxel figures and machines, pixel-art paper UI, 8-bit voices
- Day/night cycle, calendar, bills, loans and a level system
- A desktop OS you sit at: shopping, haggling, mail, bank, phone
- Named neighbours, yokai at night, side jobs and staff to hire
- Host your shop and invite friends (share the piggy bank, slap responsibly)

Runs in a modern browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari 16+). Desktop: any GPU from the last eight years, 4 GB RAM. Quality presets from Low to Ultra.
