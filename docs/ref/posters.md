# Posters and gacha cards: references and the studio system (POSTER-3)

The owner on the second attempt: "Still kinda bad. Remake from zero, research more references, and better colour."
The rules still apply: all text in English, pixel-art lettering, no AI-looking text effects (glows, gradients,
bevels, stacked outlines, crosses, sparkles, starbursts). Copy the feel of the owner's boards (scratchpad
`ref_gacha/*.jpg`: gashapon header cards, Tokyo signage), and make it cool, unique and not generic.

## Why sample.png and sample2.png failed

- **Colour.** Raw digital primaries (#ffd23f yellow, #d8402a red, #2a6ad8 blue, #2f8a4a green) with no shared
  temperature. Yellow is on every piece: title, badges, bars, hazard tape, card warning blocks. Five or six
  unrelated colours per poster, so nothing reads as printed with inks.
- **Photos pasted on.** Full-colour, full-saturation photos sit under flat graphics, so the photo and the print
  never become one object. The night-festival and Milky Way photos fight the type laid over them.
- **Too many elements.** Each poster has a headline, ribbon, roundel sticker, tags, arrows, stripes, rays, hazard
  tape and a dot screen. Nothing leads; everything shouts at the same volume.
- **Samey lettering.** Every title is chunky caps with the same one-pixel ink outline or offset shadow, smoothed by
  EPX into blobby shapes. Stock pixel fonts (Silkscreen, Jersey, Micro 5, Press Start) are mixed at random sizes.
- **Generic template.** Every poster is header, photo, info band. Every card is the same diagonal split with a
  sticker in the corner. Margins vary between 4, 6 and 8 px with no grid.
- **Clichés.** Sunburst rays, hazard tape, "NEW!" discs and "1 IN 500" roundels look like stock clip art, not like a
  shop on a Showa back lane.

## References (study only; nothing downloaded except PD / CC images we already hold)

1. **Japanese Government Railways travel posters, 1930s** (public domain, Wikimedia Commons, cached in
   `shots/.art-cache/commons/1930s_Japan_Travel_Poster_-_*.jpg`). They teach the most of any reference: one huge
   title, often vertical, then one picture and a quiet foot band for the facts. Palettes are three or four inks:
   vermilion, teal, mustard and navy on cream. The *Tateyama* poster puts a navy silhouette against a mustard sky
   under a giant red title. The *Kinkaku* and Alps posters print a black-and-white photograph and let the type
   colour it. **Taken:** big-title hierarchy, paper margins, the photo as one ink.
2. **Hiroshige, *Fireworks at Ryōgoku Bridge* (1858)**, Met Open Access
   (https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/37093, cached). A deep Prussian-blue night with small bursts.
   The festival is told with one dark field and one light accent. **Taken:** the festival poster's navy night with
   marigold fireworks.
3. **Hiroshige, *Evening Snow at Kanbara*, and Kuniyoshi, *Ghosts of the Taira***, Met Open Access. Two or three
   colours and the paper doing the work, a limited palette that still feels rich. **Taken:** paper as a colour.
4. **Yusaku Kamekura, Tokyo 1964 Olympic posters**
   (https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/5-11-july-2021/1964-tokyo-olympics,
   https://theolympians.co/2015/05/23/kamekuras-olympic-emblem-design/). A symmetrical layout: one red disc,
   gold type and white space. Photos are dramatic but sit inside a strict grid. **Taken:** one dominant shape per
   poster, and type that is small but placed with intent.
5. **Ikko Tanaka, *Nihon Buyo* (1981)**, screen print
   (https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O1161237/nihon-buyo-poster-tanaka-ikko/,
   https://www.cooperhewitt.org/2015/12/05/do-i-look-simple/,
   https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/poster-picks-steven-heller-on-5-top-ikko-tanaka-designs/). A restricted, muted
   palette with one small bright area, built from flat geometric shapes. **Taken:** mostly muted inks with one hot
   accent (vermilion on the festival poster, red pen on the henshin card).
6. **Showa and Taisho matchbox labels** (https://graphicarts.princeton.edu/2018/04/23/japanese-matchbook-labels,
   https://www.library.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/english/bunkaweek/2022/matchlabel/,
   https://phillumeny.com/2023/08/28/some-japanese-favourites). Tiny relief-printed labels in two or three intense
   inks, with Western type mixed into Japanese layouts. **Taken:** ink economy, and the printer's credit on every
   piece.
7. **Horo kanban enamel signs, 1950s to 70s** (dealer notes such as
   https://www.chairish.com/product/26908822/vintage-japanese-enamel-advertising-sign-1950s). Two or three flat
   enamel colours chosen to read against weathered wooden walls: blue and white with a red emblem. **Taken:** high
   value contrast between the two main inks, so a poster reads on the lane's dark walls.
8. **Morinaga and Glico caramel boxes (dagashi packaging)**
   (https://bokksu.com/blogs/news/dagashi-culture-japan-s-retro-candy-you-ll-want-to-try). Golden yellow with
   maroon and white, a single mascot, a strong frame. **Taken:** yellow only as a deliberate ink in one or two
   pieces (the secret's gold, the henshin pencil), never everywhere.
9. **Popy / Bandai toy boxes, 1970s and 80s** (https://www.ourstarblazers.com/vault/?cat=58,
   https://collectiondx.com/guide_machinerobo_600_series_packaging). A colour band across the box, bold numbering
   and a figure photographed on a plain field. **Taken:** the line-up as a numbered strip, and the product in true
   colour on a quiet ground.
10. **Famicom 1983-84 box template** (Nintendo's shared box and cartridge template, varied only by colour).
    **Taken:** one studio template, with each piece unique only by palette and lettering. That is how the posters
    and cards stay one family.
11. **Toshi Omagari, *Arcade Game Typography* (Thames & Hudson, 2019)** (https://tosche.net/non-fonts/arcade-game-typography,
    https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/toshi-omagari-arcade-game-typography-publication-graphic-design-231019).
    About 250 arcade pixel faces, redrawn and classified. Each game had its own lettering, drawn to the pixel grid at
    its final size and never scaled. **Taken:** a hand-drawn alphabet per title at native size; no EPX smoothing, no
    2x blocks.
12. **Namco *Puck Man* flyer (1980)** (https://www.computerhistory.org/revolution/computer-games/16/192/796), with
    Tadashi Yamashita's logo sketches. A custom logotype, one hero image and facts below. **Taken:** logotype first,
    then one picture.
13. **Riso and screen-print guides** (https://www.arts.ac.uk/partnerships/outreach/insights/how-to/techniques/how-totechniques-create-a-two-colour-risograph-print,
    https://oxfordgreenprint.com/home/design-for-riso/, https://www.creativebloq.com/print-design/three-colour-risograph-print-5128047,
    https://content-hub.dribbble.com/tips/risograph-printing/). Separate one plate per ink. Overlaps multiply (red over
    teal gives burgundy). Teal with yellow and red with deep blue overlap well, while two dark inks make mud. Dark
    inks carry the text. Leave about 7 mm of margin, because riso cannot bleed. **Taken:** two or three inks per
    piece, real overprints, text always in the darkest ink or knocked out to the paper, and a paper margin on every
    piece.
14. **Made By Smyth, *The Humble Pixel*** (https://www.creativeboom.com/work/the-humble-pixel-minimalist-posters-that-celebrate-the-backbone-of-design/).
    Screen-printed posters built from one pixel unit. **Taken:** the pixel as a printed dot, which here is the
    halftone screen.
15. **Screen-printed gig posters** (https://creativebloq.com/design/inspiring-gig-posters-1212720,
    https://www.fespa.com/en/news-media/52-spectacular-screen-print-posters). Halftone photos in one or two inks,
    limited colour and a bold single idea. **Taken:** the photo screened in the poster's inks, like the kaiju city in
    navy on salmon.
16. **eBoy, Pixorama city posters** (https://www.eboy.com/products/london-poster). Dense pixel detail held together
    by a strict palette. **Taken:** consistent pixel density, every element at 1 px = 1 px.
17. **Owner board: たまケロ / 鳥フォン / カニカンメカ / マッシュ cards** (`ref_gacha/4.jpg`, `6.jpg`). Title on a
    calm field, the hero toy big, a numbered line-up with names, and a price / N types / age block, warning, maker,
    QR and barcode set as a ruled table at the foot. **Taken:** the card information order, and fewer stickers.
18. **Owner board: 果獣ドラゴン, ケモカワ, パイプカワウソ** (`ref_gacha/4.jpg`, `6.jpg`). Each logotype is drawn for
    its series, one motif swapped into a letter, and one colour family per card. **Taken:** a lantern I in NIGHT and
    a pencil I in STATIONERY, plus one palette per card.
19. **Owner board: Tokyo signage, Super Potato, yakitori alleys** (`ref_gacha/7.jpg`, `8.jpg`). Vertical signboards,
    hand-painted lettering and red lanterns. **Taken:** the chochin-moji (lantern lettering) idea for the festival.
20. **Showa tokusatsu title cards** (Toho 1954-70s posters, e.g.
    https://japanposter.co.uk/products/godzilla-original-re-release-japanese-movie-poster-1976-b2-size-51-x-73cm-c83).
    Heavy, leaning brush letters cut on the diagonal, monsters towering over a miniature city. **Taken:** the kaiju
    lettering, and the suit figure standing over a halftone skyline.

## The studio system (tools/pressworks.py, tools/letterforms.py)

One fictional printer, **Ito Press**, prints everything. Its imprint sits in the foot margin of every poster.

- **Inks.** Each piece has two or three inks plus cream paper (`#f1e7d0`), set in `pressworks.PALETTES`. Overlaps are
  multiplied overprints. No free extra colours.
- **Grid.** Paper margin of 5-6 px on the sides and top and 11-12 px at the foot, which holds the client line on the
  left and the printer on the right. Facts sit in ruled tables (1 px rules) instead of badges.
- **Photos.** Every photo is screened in the piece's own inks: a 4 px, 45-degree clustered-dot halftone at 1 px
  each (`pressworks.halftone`). Either one ink on a field (fireworks in marigold on navy, the Milky Way in gold on
  plum, the city in navy on salmon) or a duotone (a flat underprint with a second ink screened over it: the giraffe
  in sumi over ochre, the henshin desk in cobalt over a pencil tint, the night stall in lantern over deep teal).
- **Type hierarchy.**
  1. One hand-pixelled headline per piece, drawn at its final size in its own alphabet.
  2. The studio figures (`figs`, cap 17) for every date, price and odds figure, shared by all pieces.
  3. The game's 5x7 face for labels.
  4. Micro 5 for the small print.

  All type is flat ink. Where a title sits on an ink field it is knocked out to the paper.
- **Toys.** The real renders, in true colour, standing on the print. The secret is printed as a silhouette in the
  darkest ink, with the studio's `?`.
- **Ageing.** Real paper scans via `tools/paperkit.py`, as before. No misregistration: at 158 px, even a 1 px shift
  is about ten times what a press shows.
