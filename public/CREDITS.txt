# Credits

Gacha Garage uses recorded music, sound effects, photos and a font by the people below. Thank you! Every file shipped in `public/audio/` and `public/img/` is listed with its source and licence.
Files were edited for the game: trimmed, cut into segments, mixed to mono (sound effects, radio), loudness-normalised, given fades or crossfaded into seamless loops, and re-encoded as MP3. Some sounds are still synthesised in code (`src/core/audio.js`).

## Photos (CC BY 2.0, attribution required)

The pixel-art shop flyers and promo posters (`src/gfx/flyers.js`) are made from real stock photos from Google's Open Images
dataset (https://storage.googleapis.com/openimages/web/index.html), which collects Flickr photos shared under Creative
Commons licences. The photos were resized and re-encoded as JPEG; in the game they are cropped, shrunk to pixel-art
size, colour-graded, posterized and ordered-dithered, and composited with renders of the game's 3D models and text.

### Product label photos (the label studio, `src/products/photos.js`)

The pictures of what is inside a pack (fruit, coffee, tea fields, a bowl of ramen, the cat on the cat food) on the drink
labels, Mikawa-ya's packs and price cards and Ramen Yamada's ticket machine are real photographs from Open Images, cropped
to 256 px or less, and in the game shrunk to label size, reduced to a dozen inks and ordered-dithered into pixel art. The
brands, wordmarks and mascots round them are original drawings.

### Surface textures (the texture library)

The surface textures of the town, the garage, the interiors and the props (`src/kit/photoTex.js`) are made by
`tools/make-tex.py` from CC0 scanned materials from Poly Haven (https://polyhaven.com) and ambientCG
(https://ambientcg.com), and one CC BY 2.0 photo from Google Open Images: each colour map is scaled to the game's
texel density (256 texels a metre for the world, more for things seen up close), cleaned and graded by
`tools/pixelart.py` (polish), and shipped as a JPEG in `img/tex/`; the scan's own normal map ships beside it as
`img/tex/<name>_n.jpg` (half the resolution, for the surface's relief).
